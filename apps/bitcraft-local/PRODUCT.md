# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

BitCraft Claim Monitor is for BitCraft settlement leaders, trusted admins, and active settlement members who need a reliable operations view while the game and Discord community are live.

Primary users use the app to understand settlement status, production, inventories, markets, map activity, empire data, and craft planning without jumping between several tools. Admin users also configure notifications, access, Discord bot behaviour, app popups, and planning sources.

## Purpose

Claim Monitor gives a monitored settlement one local-first command surface for day-to-day operations. It combines BitJita public API data, local SQLite history, Discord automation, browser notifications, and admin-managed planning into a dashboard that is fast to scan and practical during active play.

Success means users can quickly answer:

- What needs attention now?
- What is being produced, bought, sold, or gathered?
- What materials are still needed for the current settlement goal?
- Which alerts, pages, and integrations are enabled?
- Is the app running the expected deployed version?

## Positioning

Claim Monitor is a settlement operations dashboard for BitCraft: live enough for moment-to-moment coordination, local enough to retain useful history, and admin-controlled enough to match how a specific settlement actually plays.

## Personality

The product should feel practical, vigilant, and game-native. It should be direct rather than playful, dense rather than decorative, and confident without becoming heavy.

Copy should be plain and operational. Prefer specific labels such as "Craft started", "Missing materials", "Public board", and "Character verified" over abstract or marketing-style language.

## Anti-References

Avoid:

- Marketing-site layouts, hero sections, and oversized decorative panels.
- Generic SaaS gloss that hides the game context.
- One-note palettes dominated by a single hue family.
- Low-density dashboards that require extra scrolling for routine operations.
- Popups or wizards that appear in page flow instead of the current viewport.
- UI that implies uncertain BitJita data is more exact than it is.

## Accessibility

The app should remain keyboard-accessible and readable in dense operational contexts. Modal and popup interactions must stay viewport-fixed, preserve visible focus, and avoid trapping important controls off screen.

Use clear contrast, visible focus states, text labels for unfamiliar icons, and reduced-motion handling where animated state changes exist. Do not rely on colour alone for important states such as enabled, warning, blocked, active, or verified.
